The widget kit

Thirty-two widgets and ten principles for rebuilding the partner profile. The problem with the page today is not that it is ugly — it is that everything looks equally important. One cream card is a countdown, the next is a data table, the next is an empty state, and nothing tells your eye where to land. The fix is a semantic hue family: each kind of knowledge gets a colour, a shape, and a density of its own.

Colour does the grouping the one big idea

Seven accent families extend the existing Valentine palette — all desaturated to sit beside burgundy without fighting it. A widget declares data-tone and inherits its rail, its icon, its tint and its bar fill. No new heading text needed: hue is the category.

Ten principles why the above works

These are the rules every one of the five layouts obeys. If a future widget breaks one of them, it is the widget that is wrong.

1

Colour carries meaning, never decoration

Seven accent families, one per domain. A user learns the language in about ten seconds and then navigates by hue. Adding a seventh colour because a card looked plain is how systems die.

2

One hero per screen

Exactly one element is allowed to be huge — the days-together number, or the nearest countdown. Today every card is the same weight, which is why the page reads as monotone even though the content varies wildly.

3

Group by the user's question, not the schema

Nobody asks "show me my Basics fields". They ask "what's coming?", "what do I buy?", "what don't I know yet?". Zones answer questions; the database layout is an implementation detail.

4

Vary density on purpose

A countdown gets air; a fact table gets tight rows. Uniform padding across dissimilar content is the single biggest cause of the flat feeling in the current design.

5

Show the shape of the data

Rings, bars, radars, heatmaps and sparklines beat sentences for anything numeric. Reserve prose for the one thing charts cannot do: the agent's voice.

6

Empty states offer, never apologise

"Nothing left on my list" is a dead end. Replace it with a deeper question, a suggestion, or a next action. An empty widget should be the most inviting thing on the page.

7

Warm depth, never grey depth

Shadows are tinted with the ink colour (45,32,36), borders are warm, tints are 4–8% hue on ivory. A single neutral grey anywhere makes the whole palette look accidental.

8

Hue is never the only signal

Every coloured element also carries an icon, a label or a shape, so the page survives colour-blindness and greyscale printing. Body text stays at 4.5:1 against its tint — accents are for chrome, not paragraphs.

9

Progressive disclosure over completeness

Eleven pending guesses render as one card with a stacked edge. Edit links appear on hover. Show the next decision, keep the rest one gesture away.

10

Restraint in an intimate product

200–400ms ease-out, nothing bouncing, no confetti, no streak guilt. Motion confirms, it does not perform. The interface is the quiet friend who remembers things, not a game.
