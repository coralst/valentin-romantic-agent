Other rail entries worth considering

Occasions / date radar — a calendar icon with a badge counting upcoming dates he is watching (anniversary in 12 days, her birthday in 3 months).
Why: it is the one feature that makes him proactive rather than reactive, and a badge is free advertising for it.
Memory / what he knows — the profile panel promoted to the rail, each fact tagged with the message it came from and a confidence level.
Why: "the agent has a model of a person" is the differentiator; today it is a side panel that a viewer may never open.
Action inbox — pending things awaiting your yes: a held table, a drafted message, a cart. Badge = number waiting.
Why: gives autonomy a safety valve, and pairs directly with the consent scopes in concept 3.
Live architecture — already exists as the drawer; give it a rail icon with a live pulse dot so it reads as instrumentation rather than a debug toggle.
Why: it is the AWS story. It should look permanent, not hidden.
Gift shelf — saved ideas he has proposed, with price and lead time, ready to be ordered through a connected service.
Why: turns the integrations from plumbing into a visible outcome.
Tone / persona dial — how forward, how poetic, how much he acts without asking.
Why: a one-slider settings entry that lands the "it is your agent, not ours" point.
Guardrails status — a small shield in the footer next to settings showing Bedrock Guardrails is on and what it blocked.
Why: relevant to the security/design-review audience, and cheap — the data already exists.

If you only build one thing

Take concept 2's rail structure (labelled bands, real tooltips, badges) and put concept 1's fan-out canvas behind the Integrations icon, with concept 3's consent sheet as the click-through when connecting. Concept 4's "Play the plan" is then a 30-second demo flourish you can add to the fan-out later — same data, different layout.
