Five ways to show it — pick one

Same verified numbers in all five, same AWS icons the live architecture diagram uses, and every AgentCore product named in full: AgentCore Runtime, AgentCore Memory, AgentCore Gateway. Only the visual form differs. Tell me which one to build out and I'll delete the other four.

Option 1 · Two receipts

Two itemised bills, side by side

No chart at all — an invoice is a form every audience already knows how to read, and there is no scale to distort. Best when the room includes people who don't read charts. Weakest at conveying how big 58× is, since 100× and 5× look the same in a column of numbers.

Option 2 · Waterfall

From $24.68 down to $0.42, one service at a time

The exec-deck standard. It answers "where did the money go?" in one read, and it's the only form here that shows the two rows where AgentCore adds cost as visible upward steps. Best for a finance or leadership audience. Weakest if the audience wants per-service unit prices.

The two red steps are drawn at a 4-pixel minimum so they remain visible; at true scale they are under a pixel. That is the point — AgentCore's two extra line items total $0.026/month.

Option 3 · Multiple cards

One card per service pair, the multiple as the hero

Made for a slide. Each card is one comparison with the ratio at display size, the two AWS icons facing each other, and a bar for proportion. Scans in about two seconds. Weakest on absolute dollars — a reader sees 798× before they see $18.

Option 4 · Proportional area

The two bills, drawn to area

The most visceral of the five: two squares whose areas are the monthly totals, each cut into its services. 58× in two dimensions is something you feel rather than read. Weakest for precision — area is famously hard to judge, so every block is labelled and the ledger stays below it.

Areas are exact: 2,532 px² per dollar in both squares. AgentCore's square is 32.6 px on a side against 250 px — too small to subdivide legibly, which is why its three services are listed beside it instead of drawn inside it.

Option 5 · Dumbbell on a log axis

Every layer on one shared scale

The most information per square inch, and the only form here that puts all four layers on one axis honestly — a log scale spans four orders of magnitude without flattening anything, and the length of each connector is the ratio. Best for an engineering audience. Weakest for anyone who doesn't read log axes.

A dot left of another means cheaper. Green connectors are AgentCore wins, red are losses, and the connector's length is literally the log of the ratio.
